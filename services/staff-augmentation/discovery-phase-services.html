The Outcome

Reduce Risk Before You Build

Clarity Before Commitment

Discovery exists to answer the questions that are expensive to answer later: what problem are we solving, what should be built, can it be built, and what will it cost?

With PineSucceed, you get:

  • A shared understanding of the business problem
  • Aligned stakeholders and agreed priorities
  • A defined and bounded scope
  • Validated technical feasibility
  • Evidence-based solution options
  • Estimates and a roadmap you can plan against

Business-Focused
Discovery

Discovery is not documentation for its own sake. Every activity should reduce uncertainty about a decision the business needs to make.

We help answer key questions such as:

  • What problem are we actually solving, and for whom?
  • What outcome and success measures matter?
  • What is in scope for the first release?
  • Which systems, data, and processes are affected?
  • Is the proposed approach technically feasible?
  • What will it realistically cost and how long will it take?
  • Should we proceed, and in what order?

Our Approach

Our Discovery Phase
Process

Step 01

Discovery Initiation and Planning

Step 01 / 08

Discovery Initiation and Planning

We agree the objectives, participants, activities, deliverables, and timeline for the phase.

Key activities:

  • Discovery objective and scope agreement
  • Stakeholder identification and scheduling
  • Workshop and interview planning
  • Information request list
  • Deliverable and output definition
  • Timeline and milestone planning
  • Governance and decision structure setup
Step 02

Stakeholder Engagement and Elicitation

Step 02 / 08

Stakeholder Engagement and Elicitation

We gather information through structured conversations and workshops with the people who understand the business.

Elicitation activities include:

  • Leadership and sponsor discussions
  • Business stakeholder interviews
  • Facilitated requirement workshops
  • Operational and user observation
  • Technical and system discussions
  • Document and data review
  • Clarification and follow-up sessions
Step 03

Current State Analysis

Step 03 / 08

Current State Analysis

We document how the business, processes, systems, and data operate today.

Analysis activities include:

  • Process and workflow mapping
  • Application and infrastructure review
  • Data and integration analysis
  • Pain point and bottleneck identification
  • Manual effort and cost analysis
  • Constraint and dependency mapping
  • Baseline metric capture
Step 04

Requirement and Solution Definition

Step 04 / 08

Requirement and Solution Definition

We define what the solution must achieve and the realistic ways it could be delivered.

Definition activities include:

  • Business and functional requirement definition
  • Non-functional requirement definition
  • User journey and experience definition
  • Solution option identification
  • Option evaluation against criteria
  • High level architecture definition
  • Recommendation development
Step 05

Feasibility and Validation

Step 05 / 08

Feasibility and Validation

We test the critical assumptions that the recommendation depends on.

Validation activities include:

  • Technical feasibility assessment
  • Proof of concept or technical spike
  • Prototype or concept validation
  • Data and integration viability testing
  • Performance and scalability assessment
  • Security and compliance review
  • Findings consolidation
Step 06

Scoping, Estimation, and Roadmap

Step 06 / 08

Scoping, Estimation, and Roadmap

We convert the defined solution into a scope, plan, and cost model the business can decide on.

Planning activities include:

  • Scope and boundary definition
  • Feature prioritization and MVP definition
  • Effort and cost estimation
  • Timeline and phasing plan
  • Team and capability planning
  • Delivery approach recommendation
  • Roadmap and milestone definition
Step 07

Findings Presentation and Decision

Step 07 / 08

Findings Presentation and Decision

We present the findings clearly and support the decision to proceed, adjust, or stop.

Presentation activities include:

  • Discovery findings report
  • Options and recommendation walkthrough
  • Cost, benefit, and risk summary
  • Decision workshop facilitation
  • Stakeholder feedback integration
  • Scope and approach confirmation
  • Approval and sign-off
Step 08

Transition to Delivery

Step 08 / 08

Transition to Delivery

We hand over to delivery so implementation starts from a solid, agreed foundation.

Transition activities include:

  • Requirement handover and walkthrough
  • Architecture and design initiation
  • Backlog and sprint zero preparation
  • Team and role setup
  • Tooling and environment planning
  • Delivery governance establishment
  • Knowledge transfer and documentation

Our Approach

Why Choose PineSucceed for Discovery?

We follow a defined discovery process covering planning, elicitation, current state analysis, solution definition, validation, estimation, decision support, and transition.

Our teams combine business analysis, process understanding, user research, architecture, and engineering perspective, so recommendations are practical as well as useful.

Facilitated workshops and clear documentation help stakeholders reach agreement on problem, scope, and priorities before delivery begins.

Options, estimates, and feasibility conclusions are supported by analysis and validation rather than assertion.

Where key assumptions carry risk, we test them with a proof of concept, technical spike, or prototype rather than discovering the problem during the build.

We produce outputs that directly support decision-making and become the foundation for delivery, rather than documentation that is never used.

Discovery ends with requirements, scope, architecture direction, estimates, and a roadmap that a delivery team can start from immediately.

PineSucceed can continue from discovery into design, development, and support, or hand over cleanly to your chosen delivery partner.
